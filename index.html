THE STORY

Discover the story behind Kerala's houseboats.

The backwaters of Kerala are more than a beautiful destination—they are a living part of Alleppey's history and culture.

Among the most iconic symbols of this unique landscape is the Kettuvallam, the traditional boat that gradually transformed from a humble cargo vessel into the elegant houseboats Kerala is famous for today.

The Beginning — The Era of Kettuvallam

Traditional Kettuvallams were large wooden boats primarily used to transport rice, coconut, spices and other agricultural goods through Kerala's extensive network of canals, rivers and backwaters.

Constructed using wooden planks and traditional techniques, these boats were designed without nails in many parts of their construction.

Coir ropes and natural materials were traditionally used to join and secure the wooden structure—giving the Kettuvallam its name, commonly understood as a "tied boat."

For generations, these boats were an essential part of everyday life in the backwater villages.

From Kettuvallam to Houseboats

As roads and modern transportation developed, the traditional cargo Kettuvallam gradually became less important for commercial transportation.

But the story of the Kettuvallam did not end there.

The unique structure, spacious design and connection with the waterways inspired a completely new concept— the Kerala houseboat.

Kettuvallam began to be redesigned to accommodate people instead of goods. Simple sleeping areas, kitchens and basic facilities were introduced, allowing visitors to spend a night surrounded by the peaceful backwaters.

"The backwaters, the villages and the timeless rhythm of Kerala life."

Kerala's Houseboats Today

Today, houseboats range from traditional-style boats to premium floating accommodations with air-conditioned bedrooms, private bathrooms, elegant interiors, dining areas and modern amenities.

Yet the heart of the experience remains unchanged: the backwaters, the villages and the timeless rhythm of Kerala life.
